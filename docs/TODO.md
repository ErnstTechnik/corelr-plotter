## A four-line display (asked 2026-10-13)
A 2004 module (4 lines of 20 characters, the same HD44780 and PCF8574 backpack) instead of the 1602.
- **Porting the present screens** (same content on the first two lines, `LCDJOYSTICKUI_COLS` 20, `ROWS` 4, the row addresses of the controller): measured on 2026-10-13, **+44 B of flash and +62 B of RAM** in the serial variant, **+40 B and +62 B** in the SD variant (156 B of flash left there at that date, 509 B of RAM minus stack). The frame buffer grows from 32 to 80 bytes, its dirty bits from 4 to 10.
- **What must change:** `LcdI2c.c` stops at two lines on purpose (`#error` for `LCDJOYSTICKUI_ROWS > 2`): `LcdI2c_SetCursor()` needs the controller's row offsets (rows 2 and 3 start at `0x00 + COLS` and `0x40 + COLS`: 0x14 and 0x54 for 20 columns, 0x10 and 0x50 for the rare 4 x 16); the fixed columns of the readouts (`LCDJOYSTICKUI_X_POINT_COLUMN` ...) are laid out for 16.
- **Using the extra lines** is the real cost: about 50 to 100 B of flash per line of new content (a string in flash, its composition). Candidates: the file name and the pen on the status screen, four menu entries at once, more lines in the calibration wizard. Fine for the serial variant (1 868 B free), not for the SD one without saving elsewhere (the pen pictograms off give back 102 B).
- A full refresh of 80 cells takes about 37 ms of bus time (one character per `_Task()` call, 0.46 ms each) against 15 ms; the CPU load per call does not change.
- Suggested first step: the serial variant only, the number of rows a build option.

## Support of the Arduino UNO board (asked 2026-10-13)
The project builds for the **Nano** (`arduino:avr:nano:cpu=atmega328`). The UNO has the same ATmega328P, the same pin numbering and the same 2 KB of RAM, so most of the firmware does not change. What is known:
- **Flash:** the UNO's Optiboot leaves **32 256 bytes** (the Nano's bootloader: 30 720), i.e. **1 536 B more**: the SD variant would have room again (29 778 -> now 30 524 of 32 256). The size limits are in the build (`CORELR_FLASH_BYTES`, `cmake/CheckSize.cmake`): a board option would select them.
- **Pins:** `A6` and `A7` do not exist on the UNO (the Nano's two analog-only spares: nothing in the firmware uses them, `docs/hardware/PinMapping.md` says so); the SPI/I2C/serial pins are the same.
- **The compile-time board guard** accepts `__AVR_ATmega328P__` / `__AVR_ATmega328__`, which covers both.
- **Upload:** `avrdude` with the `arduino` programmer at 115200 baud for the UNO's Optiboot (the Nano "new bootloader" is the same speed, the old one 57600); the `upload_*` targets and `local.conf` would take the board.
- **IDE export / reference build:** the fqbn `arduino:avr:uno`, and the byte-for-byte comparison with `arduino-builder` (`tools/analysis/compare_with_ide.sh`) for that board.
- **To measure, not to assume:** nothing else differs in the firmware's timing (same 16 MHz clock); the USB-serial chip differs (an ATmega16U2 against the Nano's CH340/FT232) and does not touch the code.
- Suggested first step: a `CORELR_BOARD` CMake option (`nano` default, `uno`) that selects the flash limit, the fqbn and the upload parameters; document the one board-dependent number in `REQ-CoreLR-D-Build`.

## CPU sleep when there is nothing to do (asked 2026-10-16; not tested, on purpose)
The idea: when the step queue is full and the SD / GCode read is waiting, sleep until the next interrupt (the AVR's `WFI`).
- **Mechanism:** `SLEEP_MODE_IDLE` + `sleep`; the CPU stops, Timer2, Timer0, UART, TWI and SPI keep running; wake-up about 6 cycles, no oscillator start-up. Any deeper mode stops Timer2, hence the motors. Today `loop()` (`grbl_corelr.ino`) polls its tasks and spins when idle; nothing in `firmware/` uses `sleep`.
- **Expected gain: almost none.** Only energy is saved (not time: a full queue means no useful work), about 5 to 10 mA of the microcontroller against hundreds of mA for the 28BYJ-48 motors and the servo on the external 5 V. The CPU would wake at least every 250 us (Timer2 at 4 kHz), and SD reads are SPI busy-waits that a `sleep` does not shorten.
- **Cost:** a few tens of bytes of flash (196 B left on the SD variant, 1 868 B on the serial one); the test and the `sleep` must be atomic (`cli(); if (idle) { sleep_enable(); sei(); sleep_cpu(); } else sei();`) or an interrupt between them is lost until the next tick; every polled task would have to report "work pending"; UI reaction becomes "at the next tick" (250 us at most).
- **When it would become relevant:** a battery-powered machine, or the ADC noise-reduction mode for the joystick readings.
- Decision: kept as an idea; no measurement planned.

## A pass over the documentation once the functions are documented (maintainer's remarks, 2026-10-31) -- done the same day
The four remarks, with what was found and done (`REQ-CoreLR-D-Build-0019` for the graphs, `REQ-CoreLR-D-Build-0020` for the version):
- **Functions that call themselves in the call graph (`BlockShape()`)**: found. 11 of our functions referenced themselves in Doxygen's XML, all defined right after a line holding `__attribute__((noinline))` alone: Doxygen's code browser does not preprocess and reads that line as a call. They now use `CORELR_NOINLINE` (same attribute for the compiler, both `.hex` identical); `BlockShape()` now shows its real callees. `doxygen_output` fails on a bare attribute line.
- **The graphs as SVG**: `INTERACTIVE_SVG = YES` (the pages embedded them as pictures before, `<img>`; now as SVG documents with clickable nodes). HTML 33 -> 45 MB (`serial`), 40 -> 54 MB (`sd`).
- **The limit of the call graphs**: none had been set; Doxygen's 50 nodes cut the graphs of `loop()`, `SerialLink_Task()`, `LcdJoystickUI_Task()` (red frames). `DOT_GRAPH_MAX_NODES = 250`, `MAX_DOT_GRAPH_DEPTH = 0`; no graph of ours is cut (the largest has about 210 nodes); only the `main()` of the Arduino core in the `sd` image still is. `doxygen_output` checks the four graph settings and fails on a red frame in a graph of ours.
- **The software version, written once**: `firmware/config/Version.h` (three numbers and a suffix, `-dev` between two releases; the maintainer's choice of 2026-10-31), read by the firmware, CMake, the IDE build and `tools/analysis/check_version.py`, which the `release` workflow runs (tag = `v` + numbers, empty suffix, version written nowhere else). Any branch is "the last release plus `-dev`", never a hash. `$I` answers `[VER:1.1h.20261011:CoreLR <version>]`: the version goes in the free text after the second colon and the protocol's date stays (told to the host side). **Left**: `CONTRIBUTING.md` (lines 55 and 60, the Releases block) still says that `<version>` is `CORELR_VERSION` in `CMakeLists.txt` and that the tag must equal `v` + it: it is `firmware/config/Version.h` now (not edited: that block belongs to the release discussion); the version on the SD image's screen; a hash in the build report; putting `-dev` back after a tag (by hand).
